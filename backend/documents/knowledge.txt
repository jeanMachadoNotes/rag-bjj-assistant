Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ, jiujitsu, jujitsu, jiu-jitsu) is a grappling-based martial art that focuses on submissions and positional control.

The guard is a fundamental position in BJJ where a practitioner controls an opponent from their back using their legs.

The mount position occurs when one practitioner sits on top of the opponent’s torso, providing strong control and attack opportunities.

Side control is a dominant position where the top practitioner controls the opponent from the side.

Common submissions, attacks, techniques, or moves in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu include the armbar, triangle choke, and rear naked choke.

An armbar attacks the elbow joint by isolating the opponent’s arm and applying hip pressure.

A triangle choke uses the legs to encircle the opponent’s neck and one arm, cutting off blood flow.

The rear naked choke is applied from the back position and is considered one of the most effective submissions in grappling.

Sweeps are techniques used from the guard to reverse positions and gain top control.

In BJJ, positional hierarchy is important because better positions increase control and reduce the risk of losing dominance. Practitioners prioritize dominant positions such as mount and back control before attempting submissions.

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu emphasizes leverage, technique, and timing over strength.

Training typically involves drilling techniques, positional sparring, and live rolling.
